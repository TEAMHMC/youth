Why it works

A visit is not a program.

One assembly does not change how a young person sees their own future. Being in the building again next week starts to.

Students clock which adults come back. The first session is polite. By the second, someone asks a question they would not have asked a stranger. By the fourth, a student tells you what they actually want to do, and you can put a person, a course or a career next to it.

So everything Health Matters Clinic runs in schools is built to repeat. Same campus, same faces, on a schedule the school sets. In spring 2023 our afterschool enrichment and mentoring at 107th Street Elementary and STEAM Magnet ran four times in eleven days. That is the shape of the program.

This school year

We are on a campus right now.

Ingenium Schools has named Health Matters Clinic an approved on-site wellness partner, providing recurring health and wellness programming for students and families through the 2025-2026 school year.

The agreement took effect on November 18, 2025 and runs to June 30, 2026. Ingenium provides the room. HMC provides the staff, the volunteers, the materials and the program. None of it is billed to the school.

Three things run on that campus this year.

Quarterly

Community health fair

Screenings, health education and a resource fair, open to students, to their families, and to the neighborhood around the school.

Monthly

Wellness workshop series

A rotating set of topics. Mental health and stress management. Nutrition and food access. Financial wellness and employment readiness. Physical activity. Healthy relationships and social connection.

Ongoing

Outreach and service learning

Volunteer recruitment, student service-learning opportunities, and community drives including hygiene kits and a back-to-school health day.

One school site, at 13305 San Pedro Street in Los Angeles. Dates are set with the site principal against the school calendar.

One school, over and over

107th Street Elementary and STEAM Magnet.

The clearest picture of what this program is comes from one campus in spring 2023.

April 28. May 1. May 8. Then a fifth session later that spring. Afterschool enrichment and mentoring, delivered by the same organization, to the same students, inside eleven days.

Students from 107th Street also took part in the JREAM Foundation Youth Camp in 2023, where Health Matters Clinic delivered afterschool enrichment and mentoring. JREAM is the foundation of Kayvon Thibodeaux, a professional athlete.

  1. The room gets quiet

    Session one is an introduction. Students are polite, and mostly waiting to see whether you come back.

  2. The questions start

    Once you are a returning face rather than a guest, students ask the things they would not ask a stranger.

  3. Something sticks

    By the fourth session a student can name what they want to do next, and there is an adult in the room who knows how to help them get there.

The curriculum

We wrote our own.

In 2022 Health Matters Clinic wrote and launched its youth STEAM curriculum.

Before that, a school visit was a school visit. After it, there was a syllabus behind the room. That is the difference between a good afternoon and a program a principal can put on a calendar and a funder can look at.

The same curriculum is what lets the work travel. A science and engineering design fair at a high school, an afterschool block at an elementary campus and a summer camp are not three separate efforts. They are the same material meeting students where they already are.

Where it has run

  • Elementary school, afterschool enrichment and mentoring
  • Middle school, on campus
  • High school, science and engineering design fair
  • Youth camps and community youth events

Who delivers it

  • Health Matters Clinic staff
  • Trained HMC volunteers
  • Licensed professionals where a session calls for one

On campus

How we work in a school.

Schools carry a duty of care that no outside organization gets to shortcut. Ours is the easier job. We follow the school's rules, and we make it simple to say yes.

Before anyone arrives

  • Every HMC volunteer completes core volunteer training before any community-facing work
  • Staff and volunteers follow the school's own policies, including any clearance or background check the site requires
  • Sessions are scheduled with the site principal, around the school calendar

What we bring

  • Qualified staff, trained volunteers, and licensed professionals where a session needs one
  • Materials, supplies and educational resources
  • Registration, and follow-up after the session where it applies

What we publish

  • No student names
  • No photographs of students
  • Program descriptions, dates and locations, which is what you see on this page

The program
What is Youth Mentorship and STEAM?

Health Matters Clinic's school-based youth program. Afterschool enrichment, mentoring sessions, science and engineering fairs, and health and wellness programming on campus, built on the youth STEAM curriculum HMC wrote and launched in 2022.

What ages do you work with?

Elementary, middle and high school. The work runs from afterschool enrichment at 107th Street Elementary and STEAM Magnet through to a science and engineering design fair at Morningside High School.

Does it cost the school anything?

No. Health Matters Clinic services are provided at no charge to the school, and every community-facing HMC service is free with no insurance, referral or identification required.

Free to receive is not free to run. Materials, supplies and staffing are paid for by sponsors, grants and donors.

What is the Ingenium agreement?

Ingenium Schools has named Health Matters Clinic an approved on-site wellness partner providing recurring health and wellness programming for students and families during the 2025-2026 school year. It took effect on November 18, 2025 and runs to June 30, 2026.

Under it HMC runs a quarterly community health fair, a monthly wellness workshop series, and ongoing outreach and student service-learning at one Ingenium campus.

On campus
Who comes onto the campus?

Health Matters Clinic staff, trained HMC volunteers, and licensed professionals where a session calls for one. Everyone follows the school's own policies, including any clearance or background check the site requires.

What training do volunteers complete?

Every HMC volunteer completes core volunteer training before any community-facing work. Training and hours are tracked in the volunteer's record.

New to HMC? Take the tour first, then start at volunteer.healthmatters.clinic.

Why are there no photographs of students here?

Because we do not publish images of the young people we work with, and we do not name them. What this page shows instead is where we went, when, and what we ran.

Working with us
Can you come to our school?

Often, yes. Email partner@healthmatters.clinic with the campus, the grade levels, what you want students to get out of it, and the term you have in mind.

Can I volunteer if I am not a teacher or a clinician?

Yes. Mentoring, enrichment, health education and family activity roles are non-clinical. Clinical tasks are performed only by people licensed to perform them.

Where do the dates on this page come from?

Health Matters Clinic's own 2023 outreach log, and the Ingenium Schools agreement. Where the log records a month or a season rather than a day, this page says so.
