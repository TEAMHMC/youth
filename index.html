More than a program

There is more than one way to find your thing.

One young person may come to us because they love science. Another may have questions they have not felt comfortable asking anywhere else.

Someone else may already know they want to become a nurse, work in medicine, design technology, or help make their community healthier. There is room for all of that here.

HMC brings health education, mentoring, STEAM, leadership, career exploration, and service together in ways that give young people room to learn, ask questions, try something new, and discover what matters to them.

We come with a plan

We leave room for the conversation.

Every HMC experience has a purpose. But young people are not worksheets.

They bring their own questions, experiences, interests, and ideas into the room. Sometimes those things take the conversation somewhere we did not expect. That is not a distraction from the work. It is part of the work.

We listen for what young people are telling us, then build from there.

A conversation about stress can become a conversation about healthy coping. A question about vaping can become an opportunity to talk about the body, pressure, curiosity, and choices. An interest in nursing can open the door to an entire world of healthcare careers. A young person's idea can become a service project, a leadership opportunity, or the first step toward a future they had not imagined before.

The structure is ours. The future belongs to them.

In practice

Listening to students, building toward their futures.

Through a partnership with the 107th Street Elementary afterschool enrichment and mentoring program, HMC worked with a small girls cohort around health, wellness, personal growth, and future possibilities.

During mentoring conversations, students shared questions and experiences related to alcohol, vaping, and substances they had encountered. Rather than responding with shame or judgment, HMC used the opportunity to provide age-appropriate guidance around healthy decision-making, understanding what substances can do to the body, responding to curiosity and peer pressure, healthy coping, and identifying trusted adults.

But another powerful theme emerged.

Seven of the eight students in the cohort shared an interest in pursuing careers in nursing, medicine, or allied health.

HMC connected the conversation about health today with the futures students said they wanted to build. Students were introduced to the many roles that make up the healthcare workforce, from nurses and medical professionals to allied health, community health, behavioral health, and healthcare technology. They explored the skills and habits that can help them begin building toward those goals now.

Not every student starts in the same place

And they do not have to.

One cohort may be excited about healthcare. Another may be drawn to engineering. Another may need a place to build confidence, ask questions, or figure out what comes next.

HMC's youth programming is designed to meet young people where they are while giving them room to discover where they want to go. The tracks are not separate boxes. They intersect.

A STEAM experience can spark a career interest. A mentoring conversation can uncover a health concern. A health lesson can lead to a conversation about nursing. A service project can become a leadership opportunity.

"Maybe I could."

Showing up once can start something. Showing up consistently gives it somewhere to grow.

The program
What is Youth and Future Health Leaders?

Health Matters Clinic's school-based youth program. Afterschool enrichment, mentoring sessions, science and engineering fairs, and health and wellness programming on campus, built on the youth STEAM curriculum HMC wrote and launched in 2022.

What ages do you work with?

Elementary, middle and high school. The work runs from afterschool enrichment at 107th Street Elementary and STEAM Magnet through to a science and engineering design fair at Morningside High School.

Does it cost the school anything?

No. Health Matters Clinic services are provided at no charge to the school, and every community-facing HMC service is free with no insurance, referral or identification required.

Free to receive is not free to run. Materials, supplies and staffing are paid for by sponsors, grants and donors.

What is the Ingenium agreement?

Ingenium Schools has named Health Matters Clinic an approved on-site wellness partner, providing recurring health and wellness programming for students and families. The work continues through the 2026 to 2027 school year.

It gives the school-based work a home: a quarterly community health fair, a monthly wellness workshop series, and ongoing outreach and student service-learning on one campus, so students see the same faces over time rather than once.

On campus
Who comes onto the campus?

Health Matters Clinic staff, trained HMC volunteers, and licensed professionals where a session calls for one. Everyone follows the school's own policies, including any clearance or background check the site requires.

What training do volunteers complete?

Every HMC volunteer completes core volunteer training before any community-facing work. Training and hours are tracked in the volunteer's record.

New to HMC? Take the tour first, then start at volunteer.healthmatters.clinic.

Why are there no photographs of students here?

Because we do not publish images of the young people we work with, and we do not name them. What this page shows instead is where we went, when, and what we ran.

Working with us
Can you come to our school?

Often, yes. Email partner@healthmatters.clinic with the campus, the grade levels, what you want students to get out of it, and the term you have in mind.

Can I volunteer if I am not a teacher or a clinician?

Yes. Mentoring, enrichment, health education and family activity roles are non-clinical. Clinical tasks are performed only by people licensed to perform them.

Where do the dates on this page come from?

Health Matters Clinic's own 2023 outreach log, and the Ingenium Schools agreement. Where the log records a month or a season rather than a day, this page says so.
